# Objectives before purchases

**Current direction:** one project following the [homemade construction, intercontinental HF and autonomous station sequence](workstreams.md). Intercontinental HF is an intermediate milestone. The owner has little interest in EME; the lunar comparison below is historical and receives no further research or purchasing work unless requested.

**Status:** the owner confirmed the overall sequence on 2026-10-25. Exact hardware, budget, network size and autonomy remain open. The ambition comparison below records earlier exploration and does not override the current roadmap.

## From stretch goals to the practical starting point

This is an approximate ranking of engineering ambition, not a required purchase sequence. Satellite and lunar work are separate branches. Their difficulty depends on the selected station design and antenna site.

| Level | Capability | Proposed evidence of success | Main requirements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5. Lunar reflection | Exchange amateur radio signals with another station via reflection from the Moon, called EME | One completed EME contact with a documented station and path | Directional antenna system, careful RF measurements, very weak signal operation and a suitable site; specialized future branch |
| 4. Satellite station | Exchange voice or data with other amateurs through a satellite | One contact using this station's own uplink and downlink | Select a satellite and its operating requirements first; QO-100 needs a view of the satellite and a suitable dish and microwave chain, while low orbit satellites need pass planning and Doppler management |
| 3. Intercontinental HF station | Make direct radio contacts in Europe and on another continent, including voice | At least one European and one intercontinental voice contact, documented with the antenna and station settings | HF transceiver supporting SSB voice, suitable antenna, feed line and power, operating authorization and favorable propagation |
| 2. Local and regional station | Talk to nearby amateurs directly and through an available amateur repeater | A direct voice contact and, if a reachable repeater is available, a repeater contact | VHF/UHF equipment and an antenna appropriate to the site; actual range and repeater availability must be checked |
| 1. Receive and understand | Receive and identify amateur and broadcast signals with the local station | Repeated reception of identified HF and VHF/UHF signals with settings and antenna configuration recorded | SDR receiver, compatible software, antenna coverage and a manageable local noise environment; one band can be the first milestone |

The technical basis for these capabilities is covered by [ARRL's EME example](https://www.arrl.org/files/file/QST/This%20Month%20in%20QST/July2016/Schaefer.pdf), [AMSAT's satellite overview](https://www.amsat.org/live-linear-satellites/), [QO-100 operating guidance](https://amsat-dl.org/en/p4-a-nb-transponder-bandplan-and-operating-guidelines/), [RSGB's HF guide](https://rsgb.org/main/get-started-in-amateur-radio/operating-your-new-station/hf-dxing-how-to-do-it/) and [VHF/UHF propagation guide](https://rsgb.org/main/get-started-in-amateur-radio/operating-your-new-station/vhfuhf-propagation/).

No fixed distance or reliable contact schedule is promised. A contact in a weak signal digital mode does not establish that voice operation will work over the same path. Internet linked contacts and remote web reception must be recorded separately from the local station's radio performance.

## Intermediate target: homemade HF operation

Build and understand the antenna, receive and transmit paths, filtering, controls and station integration, then achieve an intercontinental HF voice contact. Select documented circuits, components and any justified modules or kits as the work progresses. Add VHF/UHF when the later network design requires it.

The desk holds the radio, controls and computer. Antenna placement and feed line routing are part of the station design; fitting everything inside a room is not assumed.

Follow the construction stages in the [roadmap](roadmap.md). Lunar work is parked and satellites remain unselected. Reaching the HF milestone leads into autonomous station development.

## Later target: autonomous emergency stations

The owner also requested a solar powered base left in place, with portable radios distributed to other people for communication during outages. Compare costs and the differences between direct PMR446, a staffed CB base and an unattended amateur repeater in the [budget study](budget.md). City or flat countryside are the provisional terrain scenarios. Four portables are an explicit costing assumption, not a confirmed group size.

This is the later phase of the same project. The service, site, required range and autonomy are still to be selected. A possible acceptance test is intelligible communication at agreed locations and 72 hours of measured autonomy under a defined traffic pattern; neither has been demonstrated and the duration is not yet a confirmed requirement.

## Construction objectives at every level

1. Build and characterize an antenna appropriate to the chosen first band and available space.
2. Identify one real reception problem and test a remedy, such as placement, feed line changes or a filter when justified.
3. Build an original station accessory, such as tuning controls or a measured passive RF component, with its schematic and any firmware.
4. Build the receiver and transmitter from documented designs in verifiable stages. Evaluate kits or purchased reference equipment when they support the learning and measurement goals; no full radio purchase is selected by default.
5. Record actual costs, assembly photos, measurements, unsuccessful changes and the next decision in the build log.

## What this means for buying

The initial basket should serve the first local reception and first antenna build. An SDR may remain useful later for monitoring, provided it is protected from nearby transmission. Soldering equipment and a multimeter can support multiple builds; check what is already owned before selecting them.

Antennas, coaxial cable, connectors and RF power equipment must suit their actual band and use. HF components are not automatically suitable for a microwave satellite station. A wide receiving frequency range is not a universal transmit capability.

Before choosing exact models or quantities, establish the antenna site and usable dimensions, initial budget, existing tools, computer compatibility and operator qualifications. Future transmission remains subject to the [Italian requirements](https://ispettorati.mise.gov.it/index.php/servizi/radioamatori/) already recorded in the project. No products, prices or purchases have been selected in this objective comparison.
